How this works. Every spot is checked against four independent weather models (GFS, ECMWF, ICON and Environment Canada's GEM/HRDPS, which best resolves local BC terrain). For each spot we don't just show raw numbers — we classify the hour as thermal (local sun-driven sea breeze or valley wind, e.g. the Squamish afternoon southerly), outflow (cold-air drainage / gap wind, e.g. Squamish's northerly), or synoptic (general frontal/gradient wind across the Strait). Probability = the share of models landing your chosen knot range at that hour; confidence is shaded down for pure mesoscale thermal signals that only the fine-resolution model catches, and for wind blowing from a direction that isn't clean onshore/cross-shore at that spot.

Squamish Spit and Furry Creek get an extra correction: raw coarse-model wind badly under-reads this specific thermal (a well-documented local quirk), so once the model shows a clean SW signal we scale it up roughly 2.85x, based on field calibration from a long-time local rider (West Coast Wind Sports). Environment Canada's own Howe Sound marine bulletin — generally the single best resource for today's Squamish wind — is shown above when available, and is worth cross-checking against the live wind meter linked at the top before you drive out.

This is a forecasting aid, not a safety guarantee — always check current conditions, tides and marine warnings before launching.
